## What's new in 1.5

### Folder icons now support dark mode
Dockyard folders in the Dock now switch to a dark icon along with the rest of macOS.

- **Dark folder icons.** In dark mode, a folder's tile turns charcoal, and the folder's color moves to its symbol and its Dockyard badge. This matches the way Apple's own dark icons look. Emoji, pictures and folder contents still show on top as before.
- **Follows your icon style.** On macOS 26 and later, folders follow the **Icon & widget style** setting in System Settings → Appearance:
  - **Dark** always shows dark folder icons.
  - **Automatic** shows dark icons in Dark Mode and light icons in Light Mode.
  - On earlier versions of macOS, folders simply follow Dark Mode.
- **Switches instantly.** Folder icons change at the same moment as your other Dock icons, with no restart needed. This includes switches made by automatic appearance (Light during the day, Dark at night).
- **Folders that use an app's icon** show that app's own dark icon, if it has one, and switch at the same time as everything else.
- Folder previews in **Settings** and in the menu bar menu use dark icons too.

### Good to know
- After updating, Dockyard restarts the Dock **once**, so your existing folders pick up dark-mode support. Your Dock items and their order don't change.
- Clear and Tinted icon styles aren't supported yet. With those styles, folders show their light or dark icon, whichever matches your appearance setting.

### Missed a few versions? Since 1.3:
- **1.4**: running apps show a dot under their icon inside folders, and you can right-click them to **Show**, **Hide** or **Quit**.
- **1.4.1**: folder icons stay full-size in the Dock after it restarts, instead of shrinking onto a gray tile.

## Install
Already on 1.3 or later? Dockyard updates itself. Choose **Check for Updates…** in the menu bar menu to get it now.

Otherwise, download **Dockyard-1.5.zip** below, unzip it, move **Dockyard.app** to Applications and open it. Signed and notarized by Apple.

Requires macOS 14 or later · Apple Silicon and Intel
